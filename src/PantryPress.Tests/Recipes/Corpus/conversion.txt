# The conversion table (RCP-005; PP4.8): what RecipeConverter shows a reader for lines and steps bloggers really write, in
# metric and in US measures.
#
# An ingredient case is a "> " line, the ingredient exactly as written (escapes as in ingredient-lines.txt), then four lines
# in this order: metric at 1x, metric at 2x, US at 1x, US at 1/2x. Each says:
#   <variant> = "<the line the reader sees>"     then ", converted" when any of it was written in the other system, and
#                                                ", rounded" when the amount shown is not exact
#   <variant> = as written                       nothing to convert or scale
#   <variant> = as written, unscaled <reason>    not converted or scaled, and flagged: unparsed or other-amounts
# A line that does not convert is what the scaler shows (scaling.txt), so most "us" rows of a US line are plain scaling.
#
# A step case is a "step> " line, the step exactly as written, then two lines, metric and us: the step with its temperatures
# converted, or "as written". Steps are never scaled.
#
# Every expectation here was read and approved by hand. Nothing rewrites this file: when the converter's answer for a line
# changes on purpose, that line is edited, and the change says why (CLAUDE.md: golden files are never re-approved in bulk).
# Pinned on purpose:
# - a teaspoon or tablespoon stays as written in metric; grams of an item with no density become ounces in US, however small
#   ("5 g salt" is 1/8 oz);
# - "1 cup sifted flour" matches no density (only the item is matched, and the note is never read), so it becomes ml;
# - a size in a count line stays in its system ("1 (14 oz) can" in metric);
# - punctuation the blogger joined after a unit is theirs, and stays ("1 c.flour" is "120 g.flour");
# - an amount past the stored term cap is display only ("1000000 gallons" is "3785410 liters");
# - a temperature with no scale ("350 degrees", "350F") is no temperature; a text that already names one in the target
#   system, or holds one joined to another number ("110°F/43°C") or to a hyphen ("110°F-115°F"), keeps every temperature
#   as written;
# - a temperature at or above 100 °C or 215 °F rounds to fives (180 °C is 355 °F, 238 °F is 115 °C).
