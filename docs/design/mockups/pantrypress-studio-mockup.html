    Brown Butter Banana Bread with a Crackly Sugar Top

    Browning the butter takes six extra minutes and makes this loaf taste like toffee. Sour cream keeps it moist for days.

    NBBy Nadia Brooks Published Oct 4, 2026 · Updated Oct 19, 2026 ★★★★★ 4.8 (126 ratings)

    I bake this on the Sunday after the bananas on the counter have gone fully freckled. It is the loaf my kids ask for by name, and the one I bring when a neighbor has a new baby.

    The two upgrades over a standard banana bread are small. Browned butter adds a nutty, almost caramel depth, and a spoonful of sour cream keeps the crumb tender on day three. A handful of turbinado sugar on top bakes into a crackly crust.

    AdvertisementIn-content slot

    Why brown the butter?

    When butter melts, its milk solids sink. Keep cooking and those solids toast, and the butter goes from pale yellow to amber with a smell like hazelnuts. That flavor carries through the whole loaf.

    TipUse a light-colored saucepan so you can see the solids change color. Pull it off the heat the moment they turn hazelnut brown.

    Brown Butter Banana Bread

    A tender, toffee-scented banana loaf with browned butter, sour cream, and a crackly turbinado top.

    ★★★★★ 4.8 from 126 ratings

    Ingredients

    Instructions

    1. Heat the oven to . Grease a loaf pan and line it with parchment, leaving an overhang on the long sides.
    2. Melt the butter in a light-colored saucepan over medium heat, swirling, until the milk solids turn hazelnut brown and smell toasty, 5 to 7 minutes. Scrape into a large bowl and cool 10 minutes.
    3. Whisk in the brown sugar, then the eggs one at a time, then the bananas, sour cream, and vanilla.
    4. In a second bowl, whisk the flour, baking soda, salt, and cinnamon. Fold into the wet ingredients until a few streaks remain, then fold in the walnuts.
    5. Scrape into the pan, sprinkle with turbinado sugar, and bake 55 to 65 minutes, until a skewer comes out with a few moist crumbs.
    6. Cool 15 minutes in the pan, lift out by the parchment, and cool completely before slicing.

    Notes

    • Keeps 3 days wrapped, or 3 months frozen in slices.
    • No sour cream? Use plain full-fat yogurt.
